01

What is ΔVRE?

ΔVRE — Volatility Range Engine — is a TradingView indicator that answers the single most important question in options trading: how far is this stock likely to move by a given date?

Using implied volatility (or historical vol as a fallback), ΔVRE computes a probabilistic price envelope — an upper and lower band — for four distinct time horizons simultaneously: end of week, end of month, end of quarter, and end of year. These bands represent a ~68% probability range (one standard deviation), meaning price is expected to remain inside them roughly two-thirds of the time.

🎯
Strike Anchoring

Use EoM and EoQ band edges as logical strike targets for short puts, iron condors, and spreads — the math does the work.

📅
Earnings Awareness

Bands automatically widen as earnings approach through an 8-phase ramp system — no manual adjustments needed.

💡
Strategy Signals

The dashboard reads vol regime, IVP, and band width to suggest the most appropriate strategy for current conditions.

🚨
Breach & Recovery Alerts

Get alerted the moment price crosses a band — or comes back inside — so you never miss a critical management moment.

💬
The Core Idea

Before selling a strangle on NVDA, you need to know: "What is a 1-standard-deviation move by end of this month?" ΔVRE answers that instantly — and adjusts the answer as earnings approach, as volatility shifts, and as time decays. Your job shifts from calculating to deciding.

02

How It Works

The Four Horizons

ΔVRE calculates a separate expected range for each time horizon, using the calendar days remaining to the end of that period. Each horizon is independent — the EoQ band is not derived from the EoW band.

HorizonLabelUse CaseTypical DTE
EoWEnd of WeekWeekly premium selling, straddles/strangles1–5 days
EoMEnd of MonthCash-secured puts, iron condors, monthly expiry1–31 days
EoQEnd of QuarterCalendars, risk reversals, diagonal spreads1–90 days
EoYEnd of YearLEAPS, long-dated calls/puts, annual positioning1–365 days

Anchored vs Rolling Mode

This is the most important setting decision you'll make:

⚓
Anchored Mode

Locks the calculation to the first day of the period. Price and volatility are captured at period open and held fixed. Bands don't move as price drifts — they show you the original expectation. Ideal for monitoring open positions against their entry context.

🔄
Rolling Mode

Recalculates every bar using live price and vol. Bands shift with market conditions — always showing the current best estimate of the remaining range. Ideal for fresh analysis and strike selection on any given day.

⚠️
Which should most users use?

Anchored for managing existing positions — it keeps your original reference frame intact. Rolling for daily analysis and new trade setup — it always reflects today's market. Most experienced users keep it on Anchored and switch to Rolling briefly when analyzing new opportunities.

Vol Routing — How ΔVRE Sources Volatility

ΔVRE uses a two-track volatility sourcing system. It always tries Track 1 first and only falls back to Track 2 when no IV feed is available for the ticker:

TrackSourceWhen Used
Track 1Live IV index (VIX, VXN, RVX, GVZ, OVX etc.)Built-in for SPY/SPX/QQQ/NDX/IWM/RUT, or any ticker mapped via Custom Vol Routing
Track 2Historical Volatility (HV)Fallback for every other ticker — calculated using whichever estimator is selected below

Track 2's calculation method is chosen via the Vol Estimator setting (Garman-Klass, Close-to-Close, or EWMA) — these are three ways of computing the same fallback HV, not separate tracks. The VRP Buffer multiplier applies only to Track 2, since a live IV feed already embeds the market's own risk premium.

Straddle Proxy is not a volatility source. It's a setting under Calculation Method that scales the final expected-move result (from whichever track is active) down by a fixed factor to approximate ATM straddle pricing — it doesn't change where the volatility number itself comes from.

The Earnings Ramp — 8 Phases

When earnings are approaching, implied volatility historically expands in a predictable pattern. ΔVRE models this through an 8-phase forward-accumulating earnings ramp. As each phase activates, the bands widen automatically — reflecting the elevated vol environment rather than a static baseline.

Vertical dashed lines on the chart mark each phase transition. The ramp begins roughly 20 trading days before earnings and reaches peak expansion in the final pre-announcement phase. After earnings, the ramp resets and bands return to baseline.

✅
Why this matters for premium sellers

If you're selling a strangle 15 days before NVDA earnings, the standard 1SD formula will underestimate the likely move. ΔVRE's ramp-adjusted bands give you a more realistic picture — and automatically suppress short-premium strategy signals near earnings where the risk/reward no longer favors selling.

IVP — Implied Volatility Percentile

The dashboard displays an IVP (IV Percentile) reading — where current IV sits relative to its own history. This is your vol regime barometer:

IVP RangeRegimeImplication
LowCheap volFavor buying premium (LEAPS, long straddles, back ratios)
NeutralFair volSpreads, calendars, defined-risk structures
HighExpensive volFavor selling premium (short straddles, CSPs, iron condors)
03

Risk Management Guardrails

ΔVRE tells you where price is statistically likely to be and whether current conditions favor a given strategy. It does not manage your position once you're in it. Most options trades are never held to expiration — they're closed early for a profit, cut early for a loss, or rolled into a new structure. This section provides the entry, stop-loss, profit-target, and roll/adjust conventions for each structure ΔVRE can surface, so you have a plan before you enter, not one improvised mid-trade.

🛡️
These Are Conventions, Not Rules From ΔVRE

The numbers below reflect widely-cited practice among options traders — they are not outputs of the ΔVRE model and the tool will never enforce them for you. Some structures have fairly standardized conventions with real published research behind them. Others are managed more by structural mechanics and thesis than by a single clean percentage — those are flagged explicitly. Your own risk tolerance, account size, and conviction should always override a generic guardrail. The roll/adjust decisions always require checking live ΔVRE signals at the decision point — never roll mechanically.

💡
The Universal Roll Rule

Across every structure, the roll decision follows the same logic: check ΔVRE's live signals at the decision point, not the signals from when you entered. Conditions change — IVP moves, bands get breached, earnings proximity modifies signals. A roll that was valid at entry may not be valid 3 weeks later. The signal that matters is the one showing now, at the moment you're deciding whether to roll. When the signal says "don't enter this structure" at the roll point, it's also saying "don't roll into this structure."

Settings Reference

ΔVRE organises its settings into five groups plus a dashboard and structure table reference below. Most users only need Groups 1–2 to get started. All defaults are recommended for the majority of use cases.

⚙️ Group 1 — General
Calculation Method
1 SD
1 SD uses the standard expected move formula — the ~68% probability envelope. This is the industry standard for expected range calculations.
Straddle Proxy scales the 1 SD result down (default 0.85×) to approximate the ATM straddle breakeven price, useful when you're directly comparing ΔVRE bands to live straddle quotes. Most users: keep at 1 SD.
Straddle Scale Factor
0.85
Only active when Straddle Proxy is selected. Scales the 1 SD result to approximate ATM straddle pricing. Range 0.50–0.95. Default 0.85 reflects that straddle breakevens are typically ~85% of the theoretical 1 SD move.
Time Mode
Anchored
Anchored — locks both price and vol at the period open and holds them fixed. Band levels do not move during the period. Use this for monitoring open positions — the bands show exactly what you were looking at when you entered.
Rolling — recalculates every bar using live price, live vol, and a countdown DTE. Use this when analyzing a new opportunity to see what the bands would look like if you entered right now. Switch back to Anchored once you're in a trade.
📅 Group 2 — Horizons
Show EoW / EoM / EoQ / EoY
All On
Toggle individual horizons on or off to reduce chart clutter. Each band pair (upper + lower) draws as its own set of lines. If you only trade monthly expiries, turn off EoW. If you never trade LEAPS, turn off EoY. The dashboard table always shows all four horizons regardless of which bands are visible on chart.
Show Historical Periods
On
Draws completed past-period bands over historical bars with breach color-coding applied retrospectively. Toggle the four sub-options (↳ Historical EoW/EoM/EoQ/EoY) to control which horizons show history. Useful for visually calibrating how well bands have contained price across different market regimes.
Use OPEX Expiry Cycles (xEoM / xEoQ / xEoY)
Off
When enabled, the monthly, quarterly, and annual periods reset on the Monday following each month's 3rd Friday — aligning with standard options expiration (OPEX) cycles. The prefix changes from EoM/EoQ/EoY to xEoM/xEoQ/xEoY to signal OPEX mode.
OPEX cycles produce uniform ~28-day periods (vs variable calendar months), which means band widths are more consistent period-to-period. Historical bands and accuracy metrics are restated retroactively to match the OPEX boundaries.
Holiday note: if the 3rd Friday falls on a market holiday (e.g. Good Friday, Juneteenth), Thursday close is used as the OPEX close.
Most users trading standard monthly options should enable this.
Next Period Projections (Show Next EoW / EoM / EoQ / EoY)
All Off
Projects expected-range bands for the next period — separate from and back-to-back with the current bands. Always rolling (recalculates every bar from the current close). The DTE shown is remaining days in the current period plus the full next period length, mapping directly to what you see on your broker's option chain.
Use cases: Next EoW — when buying/selling options expiring next Friday. Next EoM/xEoM — planning next month's positions while still in the current cycle. Next EoQ/EoY — LEAPS planning for the following quarter or year.
Projections are drawn as dotted, more-transparent lines to distinguish them from current bands. Treat as a planning range — levels shift daily until the next period anchors them.
📊 Group 3 — Volatility
Vol Estimator
Garman-Klass
Controls how historical volatility is calculated when no IV feed is available.
Garman-Klass — uses OHLC data, roughly 5× more statistically efficient than close-to-close. Recommended for most tickers.
Close-to-Close — standard log-return HV using only closing prices. Simpler but noisier.
EWMA — Exponentially Weighted Moving Average, weights recent moves heavily. Best for volatile or trend-breaking instruments. Note: ΔVRE automatically routes to an IV feed (Track 1) when one is available — the Vol Estimator only applies when falling back to HV.
EWMA Lambda (λ)
0.94
Only active when EWMA estimator is selected. Controls how fast the EWMA forgets older data. 0.94 is the RiskMetrics industry standard — a good balance of responsiveness and smoothness. Lower values (e.g. 0.90) make the estimate react faster to recent volatility spikes. Higher values (e.g. 0.97) produce a smoother, slower-moving estimate. Range 0.85–0.97.
HV Lookback Period
20 days
Number of trading days used for Garman-Klass or Close-to-Close HV calculation. Not used by EWMA. 20 days (~1 month) is the standard. Increase to 30–40 for smoother, more stable bands; decrease to 10–15 for more reactive bands that respond quickly to regime changes. Range 5–60 days.
Apply VRP Buffer
Off
IV structurally trades ~10–20% above realized HV — this is the Volatility Risk Premium (VRP), the premium sellers systematically collect over time. When enabled, multiplies the calculated HV by the VRP Multiplier before computing bands, producing slightly wider bands that better reflect what options markets actually price. Not applied to Track 1 IV feeds (which already embed the VRP). Advanced users only — leave off unless you understand the implication for strike selection.
VRP Multiplier
1.15
Only active when Apply VRP Buffer is enabled. 1.15 represents a 15% structural premium — the long-run average VRP across equity markets. Range 1.05–1.40.
Custom Risk-Free Rate Override
0.0 (auto)
ΔVRE uses the live US Fed Funds Rate (FRED:FEDFUNDS) for the Black-Scholes delta calculations shown in the Structure Guidance table. Leave at 0.0 to use the live rate automatically — the current rate is displayed in the Structure Guidance table header.
Set a non-zero value to override — useful for non-US instruments where the local risk-free rate differs materially from Fed Funds. Example: enter 4.50 for a 4.50% annualized rate.
Custom Vol Routing
Blank
Override ΔVRE's automatic vol routing for specific tickers. Format: TICKER:EXCHANGE:SYMBOL pairs, comma-separated. Example: GLD:CBOE:GVZ,OIL:CBOE:OVX routes GLD to the CBOE Gold Volatility Index and OIL to the CBOE Crude Oil Volatility Index. Leave blank unless you have a specific reason — incorrect entries will trigger a routing-failed warning in the dashboard.
📈 Group 4 — Earnings Curve
Enable Earnings Curve
On
Master toggle for the 8-phase earnings ramp profiling system. When active, ΔVRE analyses the ticker's historical vol behaviour across 8 pre-earnings phases and automatically widens the bands proportionally as earnings approach, reflecting the elevated vol that markets historically price in. Leave on for all earnings-reporting stocks. Safe to turn off for indices (SPY, QQQ) and ETFs that never report earnings.
Earnings History Window
5 years
How many years of historical earnings cycles to use when calculating the ramp ratios. 5 years (20 quarters) is the default — enough data for a statistically meaningful ramp profile while remaining relevant to the current business cycle. For newer tickers with limited history, ΔVRE automatically uses all available data. The curve requires a minimum of 4 quarters before activating. Range 1–10 years.
Earnings Signal Adjustment
On
When enabled, short-premium strategy signals are automatically downgraded within T-20 to T-2 days before earnings — the window where earnings risk makes selling premium unfavourable:
🟢 green → ~🟡 amber (proceed with caution)
🟡 amber → ~🔴 red (avoid)
The ~ prefix on any signal is your visual cue that earnings proximity has modified the signal from its raw vol-driven value.
Excluded structures (long premium — earnings vol helps, not hurts): Back Ratio, LEAPS, ZEBRA, Collar, Cal/Diag. T-1 and T-0 are excluded as vol is already fully priced by then.
Show Earnings Phase Lines
On
Draws vertical dashed lines on the chart at each earnings phase transition, labeled with the phase name and the active ramp ratio for that phase. Useful while learning the system. Toggle off once familiar to keep the chart clean.

Example B — Reading Signal Strength: MU (Risk Reversal, xEoQ) 🟡 Conditional Signal

Not every signal in the strategy panel is a clean green checkmark. This example uses a real MU breakout day to teach the most overlooked skill in using ΔVRE: knowing what a yellow signal means, and why it's not the same as either a green go-ahead or a red no-go.

🟡
Green vs. Yellow vs. Red — What Each Actually Means

ΔVRE's strategy signals use a three-state system, not a binary on/off: green means the strategy's full condition set is met — IVP, calibration, and regime all align. Red means the strategy is actively contraindicated. Yellow sits in between — the strategy is structurally viable and worth considering, but one or more supporting conditions (often calibration or regime confidence) isn't fully confirmed. On MU here, Risk Reversal shows yellow across EoW, xEoM, and xEoQ — not a rejection, but a signal to size and confirm more carefully than you would on a green light.

⚡
The Setup: MU's +8.5% Breakout Day

MU gapped up +$38.10 (+8.48%) to $487.48 — a sharp, catalyst-driven move following a strong multi-week uptrend. IVP sits at a moderate 52%. A Risk Reversal here would sell the xEoQ lower put ($223.32) to help finance a long call near the xEoQ upper ($628.97) — but the yellow status means this should be sized as a moderate-conviction trade, not a maximum-conviction one.

💡
How to Act on a Yellow Signal

Three practical responses to a yellow Risk Reversal: (1) Reduce size — trade a smaller position than you would on green, since the tool itself is signaling lower confidence. (2) Check the "why" — read the narrative panel; here it's calibration ("poorly calibrated — realized moves exceeding implied range"), which means the bands may be a less reliable strike guide than usual. (3) Re-check before committing — yellow signals can resolve to green or red as the next bar closes; if you're not under time pressure, waiting one more session for confirmation costs little.

Example C — META (Calendar Spread, xEoM front / xEoQ back) ✅ Clean Win

A Calendar spread sells a near-dated option and buys a longer-dated option at the same strike, profiting from the difference in time decay rates. The front leg (xEoM, ~21–30 DTE) decays faster than the back leg (xEoQ, ~84–105 DTE) — you collect the front premium repeatedly while the back leg retains most of its value. The key operational insight: never roll the front leg mechanically. At each front-leg expiry, check ΔVRE's live signals before rolling — if Cal/Diag is still active, IVP is in range, and bands are intact, roll. If conditions have changed, close the spread instead. And always close the back leg before it goes to near-zero extrinsic value — don't hold long options to expiry.

📅
Why This Setup Is the Calendar Sweet Spot

Three conditions converge on Jun 28: IVP 39% (mid-range — front premium worth selling, back leg not overpriced to buy), price perfectly centered (↑52% xEoM / ↑51% xEoQ — sitting almost exactly at the mid-band strike on both horizons), and META earnings ~33 days away (Jul 31) — close enough that front-month has some earnings premium baked in, making it richer to sell, but outside the T-20 window so the ~ modifier hasn't suppressed the Cal/Diag signal yet. Both horizons show well-calibrated vol tracking realized moves closely. This is what a clean calendar entry looks like on ΔVRE.
